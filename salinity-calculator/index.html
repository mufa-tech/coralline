How it is calculated

What SG means

Specific gravity is the density of your tank water divided by the density of pure water, both at a reference temperature. The same water reads a slightly different SG at 25/25 °C than at 60/60 °F, which is why a 1.025 from one meter and a 1.025 from another are not always the same salinity.

The equation

The calculator uses EOS-80, the international one-atmosphere equation of state of seawater (UNESCO 1981; Millero and Poisson 1981). It gives the density of seawater from salinity and temperature:

ρ = ρw + A·S + B·S1.5 + C·S²

where ρw is pure water and A, B and C are polynomials in temperature. At 35 ppt and 25 °C it gives 1023.343 kg/m³ against 997.048 for pure water: SG 1.0264. Going from SG back to ppt, the calculator searches the equation by bisection until it matches your reading.

What to aim for

Natural seawater is about 35 ppt. Most reefers keep 33 to 35.5 and care more about keeping it steady than about the exact number. Calibrate a refractometer with 35 ppt calibration fluid, not with RO water alone.

Worked example

Your refractometer is set for 25/25 °C and reads 1.025. The calculator finds 33.2 ppt, about 1.8 ppt under natural seawater. To reach 35 ppt you would top up with saltwater, not fresh, until it reads 1.0264.

The same 35 ppt on a hydrometer calibrated at 60/60 °F reads 1.0269.

These are estimates. Test kits have tolerances and every tank uses a little differently: dose in parts and test again before the next dose.
