How it is calculated

What is in limewater

Limewater is calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2, dissolved in RO/DI water. Saturated at room temperature it holds about 800 mg/L of calcium. Each calcium comes with two hydroxide ions, which turn into alkalinity in the tank: 800 ÷ 40.078 × 2 = 40 meq/L, or about 112 dKH. That is why kalkwasser adds calcium and alkalinity in balance.

Per day

Ca ppm a day = 800 × saturation × top-off litres ÷ system litres
Alk meq/L a day = 40 × saturation × top-off litres ÷ system litres

To cover a known alkalinity use, turn it around: litres a day = use in meq/L × system litres ÷ (40 × saturation).

Limits

Your evaporation is the ceiling: kalkwasser only goes in as top-off. It also pushes pH up, so drip it slowly and never dose the cloudy part. If your tank uses more than evaporation can carry, add a two-part or another supplement for the rest.

Worked example

400 L system, 2 L of evaporation a day, all topped off with fully saturated kalkwasser.

Ca: 800 × 2 ÷ 400 = 4 ppm a day
Alk: 40 × 2 ÷ 400 = 0.2 meq/L = 0.56 dKH a day

If your alkalinity falls 1 dKH a day (0.357 meq/L), you would need 0.357 × 400 ÷ 40 = 3.57 L of kalkwasser a day, more than the 2 L that evaporate.

These are estimates. Test kits have tolerances and every tank uses a little differently: dose in parts and test again before the next dose.
