Phosphorus ppb to phosphate ppm, and NO3-N to nitrate

Phosphate (PO4) in ppm = phosphorus (P) in ppb × 3.066 ÷ 1000. 30 ppb of phosphorus is 0.092 ppm of phosphate. Nitrate (NO3) = nitrate as nitrogen (NO3-N) × 4.427.

How it is calculated

Why the numbers differ

A phosphorus checker counts only the phosphorus atom. A phosphate test counts the whole PO4 ion, which also carries four oxygen atoms. The ratio of their molar masses is 94.971 ÷ 30.974 = 3.066, and ppb ÷ 1000 gives ppm:

PO4 ppm = P ppb × 3.066 ÷ 1000
P ppb = PO4 ppm × 1000 ÷ 3.066

Nitrate

Nitrate as nitrogen counts only the nitrogen atom of NO3. The ratio is 62.004 ÷ 14.007 = 4.427:

NO3 = NO3-N × 4.427

So a lab result of 2 ppm NO3-N is 8.85 ppm nitrate. Reef target ranges are almost always written as NO3.

Worked example

Your checker reads 30 ppb.

30 × 3.066 ÷ 1000 = 0.092 ppm PO4

That sits in the 0.03 to 0.10 ppm range many reefers aim for. 100 ppb would be 0.31 ppm, high for most SPS tanks.

These are estimates. Test kits have tolerances and every tank uses a little differently: dose in parts and test again before the next dose.
