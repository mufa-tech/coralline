How it is calculated

Calcium

Calcium has a molar mass of 40.078 g/mol. The rise in ppm times the volume in litres, divided by 40.078, gives the millimoles of calcium you need. Each of those comes with one formula unit of the salt:

grams = rise in ppm × litres ÷ 40.078 × 147.01 ÷ 1000 (dihydrate)
grams = rise in ppm × litres ÷ 40.078 × 110.98 ÷ 1000 (anhydrous)

Check the bag. Most calcium chloride sold for de-icing or reefs is the dihydrate (flakes, 77 to 80 % CaCl2). Anhydrous pellets get hot when they dissolve.

Magnesium

Same idea with 24.305 g/mol for magnesium, and 203.30 g/mol for magnesium chloride hexahydrate or 246.47 g/mol for magnesium sulfate heptahydrate (Epsom salt). In a mix, each salt carries its share of the magnesium.

Epsom salt also adds sulfate. For big or repeated corrections most reefers use mostly chloride and some sulfate, the way the classic DIY two-part recipes do.

How fast

The calculator keeps each day under 20 ppm of calcium and 100 ppm of magnesium, and splits anything bigger evenly over days. Raising calcium a lot in one go can also knock alkalinity down, so test both.

Target below what you have? Nothing to add. Let the tank use it up or do a water change.

Worked example

300 L system. Calcium 400 ppm, target 430. Magnesium 1250 ppm, target 1350.

Calcium: 30 × 300 ÷ 40.078 × 147.01 ÷ 1000 = 33.01 g dihydrate
Magnesium: 100 × 300 ÷ 24.305 × 203.30 ÷ 1000 = 250.9 g chloride

30 ppm of calcium is over the 20 ppm daily limit, so that is 16.51 g a day for 2 days. 100 ppm of magnesium fits in one day. With Epsom salt only, the magnesium would take 304.2 g.

These are estimates. Test kits have tolerances and every tank uses a little differently: dose in parts and test again before the next dose.
