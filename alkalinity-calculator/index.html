How it is calculated

Units

1 meq/L = 2.8 dKH = 50 ppm CaCO3. So dKH ÷ 2.8 gives meq/L, and meq/L × 50 gives ppm. 8.5 dKH is 3.04 meq/L or 152 ppm.

Grams to add

1 mmol of baking soda (NaHCO3, 84.007 g/mol) adds 1 meq of alkalinity. 1 mmol of soda ash (Na2CO3, 105.988 g/mol) adds 2 meq. So:

grams = rise in meq/L × litres × 84.007 ÷ 1000 (baking soda)
grams = rise in meq/L × litres × 52.994 ÷ 1000 (soda ash)

Soda ash is baking soda baked in the oven: it doses about 37 % less weight for the same rise and pushes pH up, where baking soda nudges it slightly down.

How fast

The calculator keeps each day’s rise at or under 1.4 dKH (0.5 meq/L). A bigger correction is split evenly over days. Corals mind a fast swing more than a level that is a bit off, so slow is the safe side.

Target below what you have? There is nothing to add. Stop dosing alkalinity and let the tank use it up, or do a water change with salt mixed to a lower alkalinity.

Worked example

A 75 US gal tank (283.9 L) tests at 7.0 dKH and you want 8.5. The rise is 1.5 dKH = 0.536 meq/L.

1.5 ÷ 2.8 × 283.9 × 84.007 ÷ 1000 = 12.78 g of baking soda

1.5 dKH is more than 1.4 in a day, so the calculator splits it: 6.39 g a day for 2 days, 0.75 dKH a day. Dissolve each dose in RO/DI water and add it slowly to a spot with good flow.

These are estimates. Test kits have tolerances and every tank uses a little differently: dose in parts and test again before the next dose.
